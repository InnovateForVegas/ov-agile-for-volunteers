# Blue Sky - Agile for Volunteers - Introduction

The casual and curious person, nascent civic volunteer, or experienced project manager are a few of the directions from which one might approach this variation of the classic Agile methodology, and so it will be important to provide high-level and medium-level introductory materials for different points in the introductory journey aimed at different personae (taking advantage of User Stories in describing these, perhaps).

Some or all of the Introductory content and presentation may be included within the general Agile for Volunteers documentation, though the goals and audience for introductory materials may only overlap the documentation audience. That is, someone visiting an Agile for Volunteers website to learn more, or stumbling upon a mention of Agile for Volunteers and curious to know more, is not likely to want to dive into documentation, though someone of a more familiar persona may be more inclined to visit introductory materials as part of an examination of Agile for Volunteers documentation more generally.

Thus we consider Introduction as distinct from Documentation, though these two definitely know each other.

## Stand-alone Ideas

There are a few ways to go here, including but not limited to:

- A one-pager to describe the what and why of Agile for Volunteers, referring to the Agile Manifesto origin but avoiding too much detail
- A slide deck or similar style presentation that steps through the Agile origin, the alleged complications introduced to Agile found in many workplace settings, and where Agile for Volunteers is different in motivation and implementation ideas
- One or more multimedia (audio, video, etc) monologues or discussions, screenshots or screen capture segments, and a general overview of the what and why
- An example initiative repository collection to enable interactive examination of workflows and use of platform tools and features (eg for a GitHub platform implementation). **NOTE** that this would require updates to track implementation details of the Agile for Volunteers workflow, tools, and methods.

The primary consideration for stand-alone introductory materials is likely that they are not bound by context. That is, we should not assume the consumer of any stand-alone introductory materials is seeking anything more than answer to “What is this?” and they may not even have asked that question (eg the are receiving a presentation or other interaction where they have not necessarily actively sought out these introductory materials). Thus Introductory materials should probably make very few presumptions about them motivation of the consumer personae.

Generally speaking, Introductory materials should not track implementation details or finer points, providing more high-level insights to whet the appetite, leaving specifics to the Documentation layer. A working example, an Agile for Volunteers Sandbox if you like, would be an exception to this notion.

## Documentation Integration

Obviously it is possible and advised to provide links and references to stand-alone introductory items no matter their form to provide a comprehensive set of resources defining and describing the Agile for Volunteers concept. As well, some extensions to the Documentation ideas:

- A checklist or questionnaire entrypoint for those getting started or AfV-curious and at a point where they would like to explore documentation
- A Forward section or chapter that provides a general overview of motivation, differences between Agile for Volunteers, the Agile Industrial Complex, and the original Agile Manifesto with descriptions of differences in motivations, intended audience, and so on.
- HTML-friendly embedded images or slides to offer some initial jargon and organizational insights, a “Where would you like to go first?” perhaps
- A curated AI-generated summary of the Agile for Volunteers documentation, offered by some modern Generative AI tools given a collection of input documents to generate a brief overview. Curated to validate the veracity and relevance of the summary information, and updated in lock step with the underlying documentation

Where stand-alone materials can vary in presentation type and style, adopting a form and format appropriate for the context of presentation or publication, integrated introductory materials obviously fit into the Documentation structure, flow, design, and so on. If there is multi-media content (ie a slide show), determine a best practice for created a printed version of the documentation, as an example something to think about for a particular consumer persona.
